Unit 1: Portrait of a Hero

1. 5E Lesson Plan Framework

Phase Teacher Activity & Classroom Process Learning Outcomes (LO) Addressed
Engage Ask students: "What does it mean to be brave? Have you ever witnessed an act of courage?" Discuss real-life heroes. LO-11: Observes social situations and frames relevant questions.
Explore Read aloud Section I & II. Highlight new vocabulary (bravery, adventurous, startled). Pair students to guess meanings from context. LO-3: Responds to literal, inferential, and analytical questions.
Explain Explain narrative flow, character motivations, and grammatical items (Proper & Common Nouns). LO-36: Applies grammatical structures accurately.
Elaborate Discuss how ordinary children can show courage in daily life (helping others, standing against bullying). LO-13: Supports viewpoints with logical reasons.
Evaluate Conduct formative assessment via oral queries and written LBA practice items. LO-1 / LO-26: Demonstrates reading comprehension.

2. 20-Marks LBA Question Paper & Answer Key

Section A: Written Assessment (15 Marks)

I. Choose the correct alternative (MCQs - 5 Marks):
1. Swami fell under the desk and used his teeth to bite:
A) The burglar    B) His teacher    C) A wooden toy    D) A ghost
2. News of Swami's bravery was published in which newspaper?
A) The Hindu    B) Malgudi Times    C) The Daily News    D) The Express
3. Swami usually slept beside his:
A) Granny    B) Father    C) Mother    D) Friend
4. The opposite of the word 'bravery' is:
A) Courage    B) Cowardice    C) Fearless    D) Boldness
5. Why did Swami feel that sleeping in the office room was a cruel punishment?
A) It was dark    B) He was used to sleeping with granny    C) Ghosts lived there    D) It was cold

II. Short Answers (1 Mark Each - 3 Marks):
6. What report about a boy appeared in the newspaper?
7. Where did Swami hide when he was forced to sleep in the office room?
8. What made the burglar cry out in pain?

III. Paragraph Answers (2 Marks Each - 4 Marks):
9. How did Swami's father try to prove that courage was more important than age?
10. Describe the nightmare Swami had while sleeping in the office room.

IV. Extract (3 Marks):
11. "Leave alone strength, can you prove you have a spine?"
a) Who said this and to whom? (1M)
b) What does 'having a spine' mean in this context? (1M)
c) What was the listener's reaction? (1M)

Section B: Oral Assessment (5 Marks)
• Fluency & Pronunciation (2M): Read aloud a descriptive paragraph about Swami's night.
• Comprehension (3M): Orally answer: "Why should we face our fears instead of running away?"

Key Answers:
MCQs: 1. A | 2. C | 3. A | 4. B | 5. B
Short Answers: 6. A village boy fought a tiger and stayed on a tree. | 7. Under the bench. | 8. Swami bit his leg hard.
Paragraphs: 9. Cited newspaper article about a boy fighting a tiger. | 10. Saw a tiger chasing him in his dream.
Extract: a) Father to Swami. | b) Being courageous and strong-willed. | c) Swami was reluctant and scared.

Unit 2: The Proud Flowers

1. 5E Lesson Plan Framework

Phase Teacher Activity & Classroom Process Learning Outcomes (LO) Addressed
Engage Show pictures of garden flowers. Ask: "Why do you think pride or arrogance is harmful?" LO-11: Frames relevant questions based on observations.
Explore Recite poem with proper rhythm and intonation. Identify rhyming words (glow / show, bright / night). LO-4: Recites and enjoys poems with proper stress and rhythm.
Explain Discuss the underlying moral of humility versus pride. Explain poetic devices (personification, rhyme scheme). LO-36: Understands vocabulary and figures of speech.
Elaborate Relate the poem's message to human relationships and school behavior. LO-13: Expresses personal opinions clearly.
Evaluate Conduct oral recitation check and written comprehension questions. LO-1: Comprehends central ideas of poetic texts.

2. 20-Marks LBA Question Paper & Answer Key

Section A: Written Assessment (15 Marks)

I. Choose the correct alternative (MCQs - 5 Marks):
1. The flowers in the garden were proud of their:
A) Fragrance    B) Beauty and colors    C) Roots    D) Leaves
2. Who came down to comfort the drooping flowers?
A) The gardener    B) The rain and gentle breeze    C) The birds    D) The sun
3. The opposite of the word 'proud' is:
A) Humble    B) Arrogant    C) Vain    D) Boastful
4. What happened to the proud flowers when the storm arrived?
A) They stood tall    B) They drooped and lost their pride    C) They flew away    D) They turned to gold
5. What is the central message of the poem?
A) Beauty fades    B) Pride goes before a fall / humility is valued    C) Flowers need water    D) Gardens are pretty

II. Short Answers (1 Mark Each - 3 Marks):
6. How did the flowers behave towards the modest plants?
7. What caused the proud flowers to hang their heads in sorrow?
8. Name two flowers mentioned or implied in garden poems.

III. Paragraph Answers (2 Marks Each - 4 Marks):
9. Describe the attitude of the flowers before the storm struck.
10. How did nature teach the flowers a lesson in humility?

IV. Extract (3 Marks):
11. "They talked of their beauty and put on airs..."
a) Who are 'they' referred to here? (1M)
b) What does 'putting on airs' mean? (1M)
c) Why is this attitude discouraged? (1M)

Section B: Oral Assessment (5 Marks)
• Fluency & Pronunciation (2M): Recite stanza 1 of The Proud Flowers with expression.
• Comprehension (3M): Orally answer: "Why is modesty considered a great virtue?"

Key Answers:
MCQs: 1. B | 2. B | 3. A | 4. B | 5. B
Short Answers: 6. They boasted and looked down upon others. | 7. Heavy rain and scorching sun. | 8. Rose and Lily.
Paragraphs: 9. They boasted of their vibrant colors and looked down on simple plants. | 10. By showing that adversity humbles the proud.
Extract: a) The proud flowers. | b) Behaving arrogantly or boastfully. | c) Because pride leads to disrespect and sorrow.

Unit 3: The Emperor's New Clothes

1. 5E Lesson Plan Framework

Phase Teacher Activity & Classroom Process Learning Outcomes (LO) Addressed
Engage Ask: "Have you ever pretended to understand something just because everyone else did?" Introduce Hans Christian Andersen's tale. LO-11: Engages with social themes through questioning.
Explore Role-play/reading of Act I & II. Focus on dialogue delivery, tone, and vocabulary (swindlers, magnificent, invisible). LO-3 / LO-8: Reads with appropriate voice modulation and role play.
Explain Discuss themes of vanity, foolishness, and peer pressure. Review verbs and past tense forms. LO-36: Uses correct grammar and narrative structures.
Elaborate Discuss how speaking the truth requires courage, especially when others are silent. LO-13: Justifies personal viewpoints.
Evaluate Comprehension check via quiz and character analysis questions. LO-1: Evaluates character actions and plot events.

2. 20-Marks LBA Question Paper & Answer Key

Section A: Written Assessment (15 Marks)

I. Choose the correct alternative (MCQs - 5 Marks):
1. The two swindlers claimed that the special cloth was invisible to anyone who was:
A) Wise    B) Foolish or unfit for their office    C) Rich    D) Honest
2. What were the swindlers actually doing on their looms?
A) Weaving silk    B) Nothing at all (empty looms)    C) Sewing gold threads    D) Knitting wool
3. Who finally spoke the truth during the grand procession?
A) The Prime Minister    B) A little child    C) An old woman    D) The Emperor himself
4. The opposite of the word 'foolish' is:
A) Silly    B) Wise    C) Stupid    D) Dumb
5. Why did the Emperor's ministers pretend to see the cloth?
A) They actually saw it    B) They feared looking unfit for their posts    C) It was very colorful    D) They were bribed

II. Short Answers (1 Mark Each - 3 Marks):
6. What did the Emperor love more than anything else?
7. How much money did the swindlers demand for their work?
8. What did the child shout out when the Emperor passed by?

III. Paragraph Answers (2 Marks Each - 4 Marks):
9. Describe how the swindlers cheated the Emperor.
10. Why did nobody in the crowd admit that the Emperor was wearing no clothes?

IV. Extract (3 Marks):
11. "But he has nothing on at all!"
a) Who uttered these words? (1M)
b) Why did everyone keep quiet before this statement? (1M)
c) What moral lesson does this incident teach? (1M)

Section B: Oral Assessment (5 Marks)
• Fluency & Pronunciation (2M): Read dialogue between the Emperor and ministers with expression.
• Comprehension (3M): Orally answer: "Why is honesty better than blind imitation?"

Key Answers:
MCQs: 1. B | 2. B | 3. B | 4. B | 5. B
Short Answers: 6. Fine new clothes. | 7. Large sums of gold and fine silk. | 8. "The Emperor has nothing on at all!"
Paragraphs: 9. Pretended to weave invisible cloth on empty looms and fooled the Emperor. | 10. Out of fear of being considered foolish or unfit.
Extract: a) A little child. | b) Fear of social ridicule and looking foolish. | c) Always speak the truth fearlessly.

Unit 4: The Miller of Dee

1. 5E Lesson Plan Framework

Phase Teacher Activity & Classroom Process Learning Outcomes (LO) Addressed
Engage Ask: "Who is happier—a rich person with worries or a poor person with peace of mind?" Discuss contentment. LO-11: Explores philosophical/social concepts.
Explore Choral reading of the poem. Analyze rhythm, rhyme scheme, and vocabulary (blithe, mill, envy). LO-4: Recites poems with correct intonation.
Explain Explain the conversation between the King and the Miller. Highlight the theme of peace and honest labor. LO-36: Understands poetic themes and vocabulary.
Elaborate Discuss why simplicity and hard work bring true mental peace. LO-13: Connects literature to personal values.
Evaluate Recitation test and written comprehension check. LO-1: Comprehends meaning and message in poetry.

II. Short Answers (1 Mark Each - 3 Marks):

7. Why did the King envy the miller?

III. Paragraph Answers (2 Marks Each - 4 Marks):

b) Why did he say so? (1M)

Unit 5: The Wonder Bowl

1. 5E Lesson Plan Framework

Phase Teacher Activity & Classroom Process Learning Outcomes (LO) Addressed
Engage Ask: "Have you ever helped a stranger or shared your meal? How did it feel?" LO-11: Observes social situations and frames questions.
Explore Read aloud paragraphs 1–4. Highlight key vocabulary (mound, hooted, ragged). Pair students to review context clues. LO-3: Responds to literal, inferential, and analytical questions.

Evaluate Formative assessment using oral questions and written LBA practice items. LO-1 / LO-26: Analyses central ideas and demonstrates comprehension.

2. 20-Marks LBA Question Paper & Answer Key

Section A: Written Assessment (15 Marks)

I. Choose the correct alternative (MCQs - 5 Marks):
1. Philemon and Baucis lived in a __ on a mound.
A) mansion    B) hut    C) palace    D) castle
2. What did Baucis place on the table for the ragged strangers?
A) Cup of tea    B) Small bowl of milk    C) Loaf of bread    D) Bowl of rice
3. When guests drank from the bowl, Baucis was astonished because:
A) It emptied    B) It refilled with milk as much as wanted    C) It broke    D) It turned to water
4. The opposite of the word 'earlier' is:
A) sooner    B) faster    C) later    D) quicker
5. What was the final wish asked by old Philemon and Baucis?
A) Rich merchants    B) Bigger farm    C) To die together    D) Travel world

II. Short Answers (1 Mark Each - 3 Marks):
6. Why did the village boys hoot at the strangers?
7. What did Philemon and Baucis conclude that the strangers were?
8. What did the poor cottage turn into by the end of the story?

III. Paragraph Answers (2 Marks Each - 4 Marks):
9. Describe how Philemon and Baucis welcomed the ragged strangers into their home.
10. Why could Philemon and Baucis hardly sleep on the night the guests arrived?

IV. Extract (3 Marks):
11. "All will be well. Your kindness makes this drink a feast to us."
a) Who said this statement, and to whom? (1M)
b) What drink is referred to here? (1M)
c) What value is highlighted in this situation? (1M)

Section B: Oral Assessment (5 Marks)
• Fluency & Pronunciation (2M): Read aloud a short passage clearly with proper intonation.
• Comprehension & Response (3M): Orally answer: "Why is hospitality considered an important human value?"

Key Answers:
MCQs: 1. B (hut) | 2. B (Small bowl of milk) | 3. B (Refilled with milk) | 4. C (later) | 5. C (To die together)
